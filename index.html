Filter math

The pitcher box says "change every 2 months" in the same breath it rates the cartridge in gallons. Only one of those can be true for your kitchen: a household that drinks two gallons a day spends a 40-gallon filter in three weeks, and one that sips a glass at dinner can ignore the calendar entirely. Filter math does the honest arithmetic: your glasses become the filter's real lifespan, the cartridge becomes the honest price of a gallon, and your refills become the verdict on whether a pitcher still fits the house. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

The three tools

  • Life plan - the filter's rated gallons and your daily draw become the honest number of days, against the sticker's two months.
  • Cost plan - the cartridge price becomes the real cost per gallon, against the bottle's.
  • Refill plan - your daily draw and pitcher size become the refills a day, and the point where a faucet mount stops being a luxury.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: days, savings and refills are planning arithmetic, not water-quality advice. Certifications name what a filter reduces - look for the NSF/ANSI listing on the cartridge, not the box art. A filter that runs slow, smells, or tastes off is done regardless of the calendar, and fridge filters follow the same gallon truth as pitchers.
